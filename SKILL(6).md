# Understand Deep Turnaround
## Enterprise Decline, Recovery, Renewal and Continuity Intelligence

# 1. Skill Mission

Use this skill to convert an ambiguous turnaround request such as:

- "Why is this company deteriorating?"
- "Understand deeply why cash keeps disappearing."
- "The company has restructured three times but nothing changes."
- "Build a turnaround diagnosis and recovery thesis."
- "Read the real organization behind the formal reports."
- "Find the hidden bottleneck, power center, and failure mechanism."
- "Prepare a CRO-level 13-week and 100-day recovery architecture."
- "Determine whether the business is viable, repairable, saleable, or should be exited."

into a structured intelligence and recovery system:

```text
Distress Signal
→ Question and Boundary
→ Evidence and Reality
→ Event and Pattern
→ Flow and Constraint
→ Structure and Mechanism
→ Human, Incentive and Power Logic
→ Failure and Cascade Risk
→ Viability and Optionality
→ Recovery Thesis
→ Governed Decisions
→ Sequenced Execution
→ Evidence and Measurement
→ Learning, Institutionalization and Continuity
```

The skill does not merely recommend actions. It explains why the organization
entered decline, what keeps the decline alive, which intervention has leverage,
and what new system must replace the failing equilibrium.

# 3. Five First Principles

The skill is anchored by:

```text
EXIST
→ UNDERSTAND
→ CREATE
→ COORDINATE
→ CONTINUE
```

## 3.1 EXIST — Establish the real situation

Root question:

```text
What actually exists now?
```

Required distinctions:

- fact vs interpretation;
- cash vs accounting profit;
- symptom vs failure condition;
- formal structure vs actual organization;
- official process vs actual work-around;
- legal authority vs practical influence;
- recoverable asset vs sunk cost;
- temporary shock vs structural deterioration;
- known vs assumed vs unknown.

Minimum outputs:

- Reality Baseline;
- Evidence Register;
- Cash and Liquidity Baseline;
- Formal-vs-Actual Map;
- Assumption and Unknown Register;
- Distress Timeline.

## 3.2 UNDERSTAND — Explain the decline mechanism

Root question:

```text
What structure, flow, incentive, power arrangement,
and repeated behavior are producing deterioration?
```

Required lenses:

- causal chain;
- pattern and trend;
- feedback loop;
- bottleneck, leakage, delay, distortion, and accumulation;
- historical memory and crisis residue;
- fear, identity, and defensive behavior;
- stakeholder game and hidden payoff;
- formal and informal rules;
- capability and coordination constraints;
- threshold and cascade behavior.

Minimum outputs:

- Failure Mechanism Map;
- Causal Hypothesis Register;
- Flow and Constraint Map;
- De Facto Organization Map;
- Incentive and Power Map;
- Confidence-rated Root-Cause Model.

## 3.3 CREATE — Design a viable replacement state

Root question:

```text
What viable operating state should replace the failing equilibrium?
```

Possible design objects:

- recovery thesis;
- revised business model;
- portfolio choices;
- capital and liquidity architecture;
- target operating model;
- organization and role redesign;
- decision-rights redesign;
- process, control, data, and technology redesign;
- capability rebuilding;
- stakeholder-confidence architecture.

Minimum outputs:

- Recovery Architecture;
- Strategic Options Matrix;
- Value-Recovery Bridge;
- Target-State Operating Model;
- Restructuring Portfolio;
- Scenario and Trade-off Map.

## 3.4 COORDINATE — Convert insight into controlled action

Root question:


- Workstream Register;

# 4. When to Use This Skill

Use when one or more of the following exist.

## 4.1 Liquidity and financial distress

- shrinking cash runway;
- inability to forecast cash reliably;
- delayed payroll, taxes, suppliers, lenders, or critical obligations;
- covenant or refinancing pressure;
- persistent negative operating cash flow;
- growth without cash conversion;
- margin erosion hidden by revenue growth;
- severe working-capital lock-up;
- unexplained cash leakage;
- asset sales used repeatedly to fund operations.

## 4.2 Commercial and business-model deterioration

- sustained revenue decline;
- customer concentration or major customer loss;
- weak demand quality or pipeline credibility;
- price compression or discount dependence;
- high churn, complaints, or trust loss;
- obsolete or incoherent value proposition;
- product/service portfolio destroys margin or capacity;
- channel, referral, or partner breakdown;
- market change invalidates previous assumptions.

## 4.3 Operating-system failure

- missed delivery or service commitments;
- rising defects, rework, incidents, or complaints;
- unstable quality during growth;
- bottlenecks and overload;
- critical supplier failure;
- fragmented end-to-end accountability;
- process and system work-arounds dominate formal design;
- technology amplifies confusion rather than control.

## 4.4 Governance and organization failure

- founder or key-person dependency;
- all exceptions return to one leader;
- unclear decision rights;
- accountability without authority;
- informal power overrides formal governance;
- management reports are not evidence-backed;
- incentives reward local optimization or concealment;
- unresolved leadership conflict;
- repeated restructurings produce no durable improvement.

## 4.5 Transformation failure

- too many initiatives, little enterprise impact;
- no benefits realization;
- strategy, budget, capability, and execution are disconnected;
- projects are late, over budget, or unowned;
- digital transformation automates broken work;
- no stop/continue/scale criteria;
- no learning or institutional memory.

## 4.6 Strategic renewal and continuity

- the current business remains solvent but structurally obsolete;
- succession threatens continuity;
- M&A, carve-out, divestiture, or sale is being considered;
- the enterprise must become transferable, investor-ready, or platform-ready;
- recovery must be converted into long-term enterprise value.

---

# 5. When Not to Use This Skill

Do not classify a task as turnaround merely because the user wants:

- routine annual planning;
- ordinary cost optimization;
- standard organization design;
- incremental process improvement;
- growth strategy without deterioration or viability pressure;
- generic leadership development;
- standard M&A integration with no material distress;
- a motivational response to a difficult quarter.

Use the relevant strategy, organization-design, operating-model, finance,
process-improvement, M&A, or leadership workflow unless a material decline,
viability, recovery, renewal, or relapse-prevention problem exists.

# 6. Professional and Safety Boundaries

This skill provides analysis, architecture, and decision support. It does not
make the client's final decision.

Always preserve these distinctions:

```text
Analysis
!= Board or owner authorization

Recovery hypothesis
!= Solvency opinion

Financial scenario
!= Audit conclusion

Capital option
!= Legal, tax, or securities advice

Workforce scenario
!= Lawful termination process

Operational recommendation
!= Safety certification
```

For insolvency, creditor rights, fraudulent transfer, regulated finance,
employment action, tax, licenses, litigation, data protection, safety, asset
transfer, or director duties:

- identify the issue and urgency;
- preserve evidence and records;
- state assumptions and uncertainty;
- require appropriate legal, tax, audit, valuation, labor, safety, or regulated
  professional review;
- do not facilitate concealment, evidence destruction, fraudulent preference,
  deception, retaliation, market manipulation, or unlawful conduct.

Protected priority order:

```text
Life and Safety
→ Legal and Fiduciary Duties
→ Human Dignity and Ethics
→ Evidence Integrity
→ Critical Service Continuity
→ Liquidity and Enterprise Viability
→ Stakeholder Fairness
→ Long-Term Enterprise Value
```

# 7. Turnaround Severity Classification

Classify before prescribing.

| Class | Name | Typical condition | Primary response |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | Performance correction | Results below target; viability intact | Diagnose and improve |
| T1 | Recoverable underperformance | Multi-period decline; adequate runway | Stabilize and redesign |
| T2 | Urgent deterioration | Runway shortening; trust or operational failure | Contain, stabilize, restructure |
| T3 | Enterprise distress | Liquidity, covenant, creditor, or continuity pressure | Crisis governance and restructuring |
| T4 | Existential crisis | Imminent insolvency, safety, legal, or systemic failure | Emergency containment with specialist oversight |

The classification is provisional until evidence is validated.

## 7.1 Urgency lens

These are management triage signals, not universal legal thresholds:

```text
Cash visibility < 2 weeks
→ Immediate cash and authority war room

Cash forecast covers 2–13 weeks
→ Critical stabilization mode

Runway > 13 weeks but deteriorating
→ Standard enterprise turnaround mode

No credible cash or obligation data
→ Evidence emergency; establish truth first
```

## 7.2 Viability states

Also classify the enterprise as:

| State | Meaning | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| V1 Viable | Core economics and market remain sound | Repair and accelerate |
| V2 Conditionally viable | Viable only after material changes | Restructure and test milestones |
| V3 Option-dependent | Survival depends on financing, sale, partner, or asset action | Preserve optionality and run scenarios |
| V4 Non-viable as configured | Current structure cannot create durable value | Redesign, carve out, sell, wind down, or transform |
| V5 Unknown | Evidence is insufficient | Do not overcommit; establish facts |

# 8. Deep Turnaround Question Architecture

## L00 — Purpose and Mandate

```text
Why is turnaround being considered?
What must be protected?
What outcome is required?
Who owns the decision?
What is the time horizon?
What constraints cannot be violated?
```

Outputs:

- Turnaround Mandate;
- Protected-Value Register;
- Decision-Owner Map;
- Success and Failure Definition.

## L01 — Meta-Question and Framing

```text
Are we solving the right problem?
Who framed the problem and why?
What is being avoided or protected?
What question would expose the uncomfortable reality?
What evidence would invalidate the current narrative?
```

Outputs:

- Question Architecture;
- Framing Alternatives;
- Assumption Register.

## L02 — Boundary and Unit of Analysis

```text
Which enterprise, legal entity, business unit, site, product, customer,
value stream, period, and currency are in scope?
What is outside the boundary but causally relevant?
```

Outputs:

- System Boundary;
- Entity and Scope Map;
- Analysis Unit Definition.

## L03 — Reality and Evidence

```text
What is fact?
What is directly observed?
What is reported but unverified?
What is inferred?
What is missing?
What is deliberately hidden or politically filtered?
```

Outputs:

- Reality Baseline;
- Evidence Register;
- Data Reliability Score;
- Unknown and Contradiction Log.

## L04 — Events and Timeline

```text
What happened, when, where, and to whom?
What changed first?
What triggered acceleration?

What delayed consequences are arriving now?
```

Outputs:

- Distress Timeline;

## L05 — Patterns and Trajectory

Outputs:

# 9. De Facto Turnaround Intelligence

Never diagnose only from:

```text
Org Chart
Policy
Job Description
Budget
KPI Dashboard
Management Presentation
```

Read the real operating system:

```text
DE FACTO ORGANIZATION
=
Historical Memory
× Psychological Logic
× Hidden Payoff
× Informal Power
× Real Rules
× Information Position
× Resource Control
× Trust Network
× Capability Constraint
× Coordination Pattern
× Repeated Behavior
```

Master de facto question:

```text
What past event created which fear,
which fear created which payoff,
which payoff created which strategy,
which strategy created which informal rule,
and which informal rule now reproduces decline?
```

Required comparison:

| Formal system | Actual system |
|---|---|
| Official decision right | Real approver or veto holder |
| Published process | Actual work-around |
| Reported KPI | Behavior used to manufacture the KPI |
| Budget owner | Person controlling practical access to money |
| Accountability | Person blamed vs person able to act |
| Policy | Exceptions, precedents, and unwritten norms |

# 10. Causal Hypothesis Protocol

Do not present root causes as certainty without testing.

For each hypothesis record:

```text
Hypothesis ID
Observed symptom
Proposed mechanism
Supporting evidence
Contradictory evidence
Confidence level
What would falsify it
Test or analysis required
Decision relevance
Owner
Status
```

Confidence scale:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| C0 | Speculation; no supporting evidence |
| C1 | Plausible; limited evidence |
| C2 | Supported by multiple observations |
| C3 | Strong evidence and mechanism fit |
| C4 | Validated through test, intervention, or independent evidence |

Use causal language precisely:

```text
Trigger
!= Root Cause

Correlation
!= Mechanism

Management explanation
!= Evidence

Repeated pattern
!= Inevitable outcome
```

# 12. Turnaround Runtime

Use seven governed phases. Compress or expand based on severity.

## Phase 0 — Mobilize and Protect

Objectives:

- establish mandate and authority;
- protect life, safety, legal duties, evidence, critical service, and cash;
- freeze uncontrolled commitments;
- create a decision and communication channel.

Outputs:

- Emergency Mandate;
- Protected-Priority List;
- Cash and Risk War Room;
- Evidence Preservation Notice;
- Initial Stakeholder Map.

## Phase 1 — Establish Truth

Objectives:

- build cash, operational, commercial, legal, and organizational truth;
- identify contradictions and unknowns;
- classify severity and viability.

Outputs:

- Reality Baseline;
- 13-Week Cash Forecast;
- Distress Timeline;
- Severity and Viability Classification;
- Initial Failure Hypotheses.

## Phase 2 — Stabilize

Objectives:

- stop cash, service, trust, quality, and risk deterioration;
- protect critical customers, people, suppliers, systems, and licenses;
- reduce decision latency.

Outputs:

- Stabilization Plan;
- Payment and Resource Priorities;
- Critical-Control Plan;
- Communication Protocol;
- Daily and Weekly Review Cadence.

## Phase 3 — Restructure

Objectives:

- repair business economics, cost structure, portfolio, organization,
  governance, capital structure, processes, and capabilities;
- remove the mechanisms reproducing decline.

Outputs:

- Recovery Thesis;
- Restructuring Portfolio;
- Governance and Operating-Model Redesign;
- Capital and Stakeholder Options;
- Decision Packets.

## Phase 4 — Renew

Objectives:

- restore customer value, strategic relevance, growth quality, capability,
  trust, and enterprise value;
- test new economics and operating model.

Outputs:

- Strategic Renewal Plan;
- Business-Model Tests;
- Capability Rebuild;
- Commercial and Service Recovery;
- Value-Recovery Dashboard.

## Phase 5 — Institutionalize

Objectives:

- transfer knowledge from people into the enterprise;
- embed rules, controls, evidence, metrics, decision rights, and learning;
- remove heroic dependency.

Outputs:

- Institutionalization Plan;
- SOP/Control/Decision Memory;
- Capability Transfer and Certification;
- Succession and Backup Roles;
- Relapse Controls.

## Phase 6 — Exit Turnaround Mode

Objectives:

- confirm viability, stability, control, capability, and governance;
- transition from crisis cadence to normal governance;
- preserve early-warning and relapse mechanisms.

Outputs:

- Exit Assessment;
- Benefits and Value Realization Review;
- Residual Risk Register;
- Post-Turnaround Governance Plan;
- Continuity Review.

# 18. Quality Gates

## G0 — Mandate and Safety Gate

Pass only when:

- decision owner and authority are clear;
- protected priorities are defined;
- urgent safety, legal, evidence, and cash risks are contained.

## G1 — Reality Gate

Pass only when:

- cash and major obligations are visible;
- baseline data is sufficiently reliable;
- contradictions and unknowns are explicit;
- severity is classified.

## G2 — Mechanism Gate

Pass only when:

- symptoms are separated from mechanisms;
- causal hypotheses have evidence and falsification tests;
- flow, structure, incentive, power, and time are integrated.

## G3 — Viability and Option Gate

Pass only when:

- no-action and downside scenarios exist;
- recovery assumptions are explicit;
- strategic, capital, sale, partner, and exit options are compared;
- optionality and expiry dates are visible.

## G4 — Recovery Design Gate

Pass only when:

- the target state addresses the diagnosed mechanism;
- sequence, resources, owners, dependencies, and trade-offs are clear;
- benefits and risks are quantified where possible.

## G5 — Execution Readiness Gate

Pass only when:

- authority, decision rights, funding, capability, controls, evidence, and cadence exist;
- initiatives have stop/continue/scale rules;
- critical dependencies are resolved or actively governed.

## G6 — Institutionalization Gate

Pass only when:

- critical capability no longer depends on one person;
- evidence, controls, decisions, and learning are embedded;
- relapse indicators and escalation paths exist;
- turnaround exit criteria are met.

# 20. Failure Modes of Turnaround Analysis

## FM01 — Treating symptoms as causes

Example:

```text
Revenue falls
→ conclude sales is weak
```

Possible deeper mechanisms:

```text
Trust loss
Pricing mismatch
Capacity failure
Channel collapse
Product obsolescence
Management-report distortion
```

## FM02 — Cost cutting before understanding value creation

Risk:

- destroys revenue capacity;
- weakens control;
- causes service failure;
- drives out critical talent;
- creates false short-term improvement.

## FM03 — Using accounting profit as liquidity truth

Risk:

- ignores timing, working capital, restricted cash, debt service, and obligations.

## FM04 — Believing the formal organization

Risk:

- misses informal vetoes, founder dependency, work-arounds, hidden control, and fear.

## FM05 — One-cause explanation

Risk:

- complex decline is reduced to "people", "culture", "strategy", or "cash" alone.

## FM06 — Initiative overload

Risk:

- scarce management attention is fragmented;
- stabilization and renewal compete;
- no workstream reaches impact.

## FM07 — Strategy without authority and capital

Risk:

- the plan is not executable.

## FM08 — Financial restructuring without operating repair

Risk:

- new money funds the old failure mechanism.

## FM09 — Technology-first turnaround

Risk:

- broken decisions and flows are digitized rather than repaired.

## FM10 — Ignoring human fear, identity, and trust

Risk:

- hidden resistance defeats formal design.

## FM11 — No falsification

Risk:

- management narrative becomes unquestioned truth.

## FM12 — Declaring victory too early

Risk:

- temporary cash or earnings improvement hides unresolved dependency and relapse.

# 21. Response Protocol

When this skill is triggered, use the following sequence.

## Step 1 — Frame the situation

State:

- system boundary;
- severity and urgency;
- known facts;
- assumptions and unknowns;
- decision required;
- protected priorities.

Do not ask unnecessary questions when a best-effort diagnosis can be produced
from available material. Use explicit assumptions and evidence gaps.

## Step 2 — Separate symptoms from mechanisms

Present:

```text
Observed symptoms
→ recurring patterns
→ flows and constraints
→ structures
→ mechanisms
→ incentives and power
→ failure trajectory
```

## Step 3 — Identify immediate containment

Prioritize:

```text
Safety and law
→ evidence
→ critical service
→ cash
→ trust
→ operational control
```

## Step 4 — Produce a recovery thesis

The thesis must state:

```text
The enterprise is declining because...
The mechanism is sustained by...
The immediate threat is...
The highest-leverage intervention is...
Recovery is viable if...
Recovery fails if...
```

## Step 5 — Compare options

At minimum:

- no action;
- stabilize only;
- operational turnaround;
- strategic/business-model renewal;
- capital restructuring;
- divestiture, partnership, sale, or wind-down where relevant.

## Step 6 — Sequence actions

Separate:

- now;
- next 13 weeks;
- 100 days;
- 4–24 months.

## Step 7 — Attach governance and evidence

Every material action requires:

- owner;
- authority;
- resource;
- milestone;
- measure;
- evidence;
- risk/control;
- escalation;
- decision gate.

## Step 8 — Close with continuity

Explain:

- what must be institutionalized;
- what capability must transfer;
- what relapse signals must remain visible;
- what conditions allow exit from turnaround mode.

# 23. Tool and Source Use

When materials are supplied:

- inspect current files before relying on memory;
- reconcile conflicting numbers and versions;
- cite evidence and preserve traceability;
- distinguish document date from the period described;
- inspect spreadsheets at formula, range, and source level when relevant;
- inspect visual PDFs/slides when charts or diagrams carry meaning;
- use current authoritative sources for laws, regulations, market facts, public
  companies, financing conditions, and professional standards;
- do not fabricate missing data.

For quantitative work:

- state formulas and units;
- reconcile totals;
- show assumptions;
- use scenarios rather than false precision;
- preserve the currency explicitly provided by the user.

# 26. Final Principle

```text
Do not repair the visible symptom
while preserving the structure that produced it.
```

The final objective is not merely survival.

```text
True Turnaround
=
Survive
→ Stabilize
→ Restructure
→ Renew
→ Institutionalize
→ Continue
```

A turnaround is complete only when the organization can create value, govern
risk, make decisions, execute, learn, and continue without returning to the old
failure equilibrium.

# Understand Deep Turnaround

Enterprise decline, recovery, renewal, and continuity intelligence.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Use this skill to convert an ambiguous distress signal into a defensible diagnosis
and a governed recovery direction.

```text
Distress Signal
→ Mandate and Boundary
→ Evidence and Reality
→ Cash and Viability
→ Pattern and Flow
→ Structure and Mechanism
→ Human, Incentive, and Power Logic
→ Risk and Optionality
→ Recovery Thesis
→ Governed Execution Direction
→ Learning and Continuity
```

The skill must answer five questions:

```text
1. What is actually happening?
2. What mechanism is producing deterioration?
3. Is the enterprise viable, conditionally viable, option-dependent, or non-viable?
4. What recovery state should replace the failing equilibrium?
5. What must be governed, executed, learned, and institutionalized to prevent relapse?
```

The skill does not merely recommend actions. It explains why decline emerged,
what keeps it alive, where leverage exists, what must be protected immediately,
and what system must replace the current failure equilibrium.

### 1.1 Core definition

```text
UNDERSTAND DEEP TURNAROUND
=
The capability to see, verify, explain, model, and redesign
an organization's real decline-and-recovery system
across cash, value, flow, structure, behavior, power,
governance, time, risk, execution, learning, and continuity.
```

Turnaround is not synonymous with:

```text
Cost Cutting
Layoffs
Refinancing
Replacing the CEO
Installing Software
Writing New SOPs
Launching a Transformation Program
Producing a Strategy Deck
```

These may be interventions. A true turnaround restores:

```text
Viability
+ Liquidity
+ Customer Value
+ Operating Reliability
+ Decision Legitimacy
+ Stakeholder Confidence
+ Enterprise Capability
+ Adaptability
+ Continuity
```

## 2. Trigger Router

### 2.1 Use this skill when

Use when one or more conditions are material.

#### Liquidity and financial distress

- shrinking or unknown cash runway;
- unreliable cash forecasting;
- delayed payroll, taxes, suppliers, lenders, or critical obligations;
- covenant, refinancing, or creditor pressure;
- persistent negative operating cash flow;
- growth without cash conversion;
- margin deterioration hidden by revenue growth;
- severe working-capital lock-up;
- unexplained cash leakage;
- repeated asset sales used to fund operations.

#### Commercial and business-model deterioration

- sustained revenue decline;
- customer concentration or loss of major accounts;
- weak demand quality or unreliable pipeline;
- price compression or discount dependence;
- high churn, complaint, or trust loss;
- obsolete or incoherent value proposition;
- portfolio items consume margin or capacity without strategic value;
- channel, referral, or partner breakdown;
- market change invalidates the existing business model.

#### Operating-system failure

- missed delivery or service commitments;
- rising defects, incidents, complaints, or rework;
- unstable quality during growth;
- persistent bottlenecks, overload, and delay;
- critical supplier failure;
- fragmented end-to-end accountability;
- work-arounds dominate formal processes;
- technology amplifies confusion rather than control.

#### Governance and organization failure

- founder or key-person dependency;
- all exceptions return to one leader;
- unclear decision rights;
- accountability without authority;
- informal power overrides formal governance;
- reports are not evidence-backed;
- incentives reward concealment or local optimization;
- unresolved leadership conflict;
- repeated restructurings produce no durable improvement.

#### Transformation failure

- too many initiatives and little enterprise impact;
- no benefits realization;
- strategy, budget, capability, and execution are disconnected;
- projects are late, over budget, or unowned;
- digital transformation automates broken work;
- no stop, continue, or scale criteria;
- no learning or institutional memory.

#### Strategic renewal and continuity

- the enterprise remains solvent but is structurally obsolete;
- succession threatens continuity;
- sale, M&A, carve-out, divestiture, or wind-down is under consideration;
- the enterprise must become transferable, investor-ready, or platform-ready;
- recovery must be converted into long-term enterprise value.

### 2.2 Do not use this skill when

Do not classify a task as turnaround merely because the user requests:

- routine annual planning;
- ordinary cost optimization;
- standard organization design;
- incremental process improvement;
- growth strategy with no material deterioration or viability pressure;
- generic leadership development;
- standard M&A integration with no distress;
- a motivational response to a difficult quarter.

Use the relevant strategy, organization-design, operating-model, finance,
process-improvement, M&A, or leadership workflow unless a material decline,
viability, recovery, renewal, or relapse-prevention problem exists.

### 2.3 Relationship to the `turnaround` skill

```text
understand-deep-turnaround
=
Deep diagnosis
+ mechanism discovery
+ de facto intelligence
+ hypothesis testing
+ viability classification
+ recovery framing
```

```text
turnaround
=
Recovery management
+ 13-week control
+ workstream execution
+ governance cadence
+ control tower
+ benefits realization
+ exit management
```

Routing rule:

```text
Problem unclear, repeated failure, hidden dynamics
→ use understand-deep-turnaround

Mechanism sufficiently clear and execution is required
→ use turnaround

Both are required
→ understand-deep-turnaround first
→ turnaround second
```

## 3. Professional and Safety Boundaries

This skill provides analysis, architecture, and decision support. It does not
make the client's final decision.

Preserve these distinctions:

```text
Analysis
!= Board or owner authorization

Recovery hypothesis
!= Solvency opinion

Financial scenario
!= Audit conclusion

Capital option
!= Legal, tax, securities, or valuation advice

Workforce scenario
!= Lawful termination process

Operational recommendation
!= Safety certification
```

For insolvency, creditor rights, fraudulent transfer, regulated finance,
employment action, tax, licenses, litigation, data protection, safety, asset
transfer, or director duties:

- identify the issue and urgency;
- preserve evidence and records;
- state assumptions and uncertainty;
- require appropriate legal, tax, audit, valuation, labor, safety, or regulated
  professional review;
- do not facilitate concealment, evidence destruction, fraudulent preference,
  deception, retaliation, market manipulation, or unlawful conduct.

Protected priority order:

```text
Life and Safety
→ Legal and Fiduciary Duties
→ Human Dignity and Ethics
→ Evidence Integrity
→ Critical Service Continuity
→ Liquidity and Enterprise Viability
→ Stakeholder Fairness
→ Long-Term Enterprise Value
```

## 4. Input Contract

### 4.1 Minimum input

Collect or infer the following before issuing a high-confidence diagnosis:

```text
Decision owner
Entity and scope
Reason turnaround is being considered
Primary distress signals
Decision deadline
Time horizon
Protected values and non-negotiable constraints
Current unrestricted cash
Major obligations and due dates
Recent financial history
Revenue and customer breakdown
Critical operating flows
Organization and decision map
Known legal, regulatory, and safety constraints
Available evidence and source quality
Required output and decision use
```

### 4.2 Preferred supporting evidence

- bank statements and cash position;
- 13-week or near-term cash forecast;
- income statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow history;
- receivables, payables, debt, covenant, tax, and payroll schedules;
- customer, product, channel, and contract data;
- capacity, quality, incident, and service-performance data;
- organization chart, role charters, decision rights, and meeting records;
- operating procedures, system extracts, and audit trails;
- customer, supplier, lender, employee, and stakeholder evidence;
- transformation portfolio, project status, and benefit records;
- legal, regulatory, safety, and continuity materials.

### 4.3 Missing-data protocol

When material evidence is missing:

```text
Classify viability as V5 — Unknown
→ Separate fact, observation, derivation, hypothesis, assumption, and unknown
→ Produce only a provisional diagnosis
→ Identify decisions that cannot safely be made
→ Create an Evidence Acquisition Plan
→ Define a deadline for reclassification
```

Never fabricate missing figures. Use scenarios, ranges, and explicit assumptions.

## 5. Operating Modes

Select the lightest mode capable of supporting the decision.

| Mode | Name | Use when | Minimum output |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Rapid Triage | Urgency is high or evidence is thin | Severity, viability, immediate containment, evidence gaps |
| M2 | Deep Diagnosis | Mechanism is unclear or failure repeats | Failure mechanism, flow, de facto organization, causal hypotheses |
| M3 | Recovery Architecture | Diagnosis is sufficient to design choices | Recovery thesis, options, target state, sequencing |
| M4 | Execution Handoff | Recovery direction is chosen | Workstreams, governance, 13-week/100-day handoff to `turnaround` |
| M5 | Board / Owner Decision Support | Irreversible or material decision is required | Decision packet, options, trade-offs, authority, deadline, conditions |

### 5.1 Mode-selection rule

```text
Immediate safety, legal, payroll, creditor, or cash threat
→ M1 first

Repeated decline or contradictory narratives
→ M2

Mechanism sufficiently supported and design decision required
→ M3

Approved recovery direction and execution control required
→ M4

Sale, financing, closure, leadership, capital, or major restructuring decision
→ M5
```

### 5.2 Stop rule

Do not continue adding analytical depth when:

- the decision is already supported by sufficient evidence;
- additional analysis will not change the decision;
- the cost of delay exceeds the value of information;
- safety, legal, or liquidity containment must occur first;
- the user requested a bounded output.

## 6. Five First Principles

The skill is anchored by:

```text
EXIST
→ UNDERSTAND
→ CREATE
→ COORDINATE
→ CONTINUE
```

### 6.1 EXIST — Establish the real situation

Root question:

```text
What actually exists now?
```

Required distinctions:

- fact vs interpretation;
- cash vs accounting profit;
- symptom vs failure condition;
- formal structure vs actual organization;
- official process vs work-around;
- legal authority vs practical influence;
- recoverable asset vs sunk cost;
- temporary shock vs structural deterioration;
- known vs assumed vs unknown.

Minimum outputs:

- Reality Baseline;
- Evidence Register;
- Cash and Liquidity Baseline;
- Formal-vs-Actual Map;
- Assumption and Unknown Register;
- Distress Timeline.

### 6.2 UNDERSTAND — Explain the decline mechanism

Root question:

```text
What structure, flow, incentive, power arrangement,
and repeated behavior are producing deterioration?
```

Required lenses:

- causal chain;
- pattern and trend;
- feedback loop;
- bottleneck, leakage, delay, distortion, and accumulation;
- historical memory and crisis residue;
- fear, identity, and defensive behavior;
- stakeholder game and hidden payoff;
- formal and informal rules;
- capability and coordination constraints;
- threshold and cascade behavior.

Minimum outputs:

- Failure Mechanism Map;
- Causal Hypothesis Register;
- Flow and Constraint Map;
- De Facto Organization Map;
- Incentive and Power Map;
- Confidence-rated Root-Cause Model.

### 6.3 CREATE — Design a viable replacement state

Root question:

```text
What viable operating state should replace the failing equilibrium?
```

Possible design objects:

- recovery thesis;
- revised business model;
- portfolio choices;
- capital and liquidity architecture;
- target operating model;
- organization and role redesign;
- decision-rights redesign;
- process, control, data, and technology redesign;
- capability rebuilding;
- stakeholder-confidence architecture.

Minimum outputs:

- Recovery Architecture;
- Strategic Options Matrix;
- Value-Recovery Bridge;
- Target-State Operating Model;
- Restructuring Portfolio;
- Scenario and Trade-off Map.

### 6.4 COORDINATE — Convert insight into controlled action

Root question:

```text
Who decides, who executes, with what authority, resources,
controls, evidence, cadence, and escalation path?
```

Minimum outputs:

- Turnaround Mandate;
- Recovery Governance Charter;
- Decision Catalogue;
- Delegation of Authority;
- Recovery Office or Transformation Office design;
- Workstream Register;
- Dependency and Escalation Map;
- 13-week and 100-day cadence;
- Stakeholder Communication Plan.

### 6.5 CONTINUE — Prevent relapse and preserve value

Root question:

```text
How will the organization retain gains, learn, adapt,
transfer capability, and avoid the old equilibrium?
```

Minimum outputs:

- Institutionalization Plan;
- Control Sustainment Plan;
- Capability Transfer Plan;
- Succession and Backup Architecture;
- Learning and Decision Memory;
- Exit-from-Turnaround Criteria;
- Post-recovery governance cadence;
- Continuity and resilience design.

## 7. Core Diagnostic Runtime

Run these eight stages in order. Optional lenses in Section 8 deepen selected
stages; they do not replace this core runtime.

### Stage 1 — Mandate and Boundary

Questions:

```text
Why is turnaround being considered?
What must be protected?
Who owns the decision?
What entity, business, geography, process, or time period is in scope?
What is out of scope?
What constraints cannot be violated?
What decision is required, and by when?
```

Outputs:

- Turnaround Mandate;
- System Boundary;
- Decision-Owner Map;
- Protected-Value Register;
- Success and Failure Definition.

Exit condition:

```text
A named decision owner, bounded system, required decision,
and explicit deadline exist.
```

### Stage 2 — Reality and Evidence

Questions:

```text
What is fact?
What is directly observed?
What is derived?
What is hypothesis or assumption?
What is unknown?
What changed, when, and by how much?
Where do formal reports conflict with operational reality?
```

Outputs:

- Reality Baseline;
- Evidence Register;
- Distress Timeline;
- Formal-vs-Actual Map;
- Unknown and Assumption Register.

Exit condition:

```text
Material claims are traceable to evidence or explicitly classified as uncertain.
```

### Stage 3 — Cash and Viability

Questions:

```text
How much unrestricted cash exists?
Which obligations are due, when, and to whom?
What cash is trapped, disputed, restricted, or unreliable?
What actions extend or shorten runway?
Does the core business generate contribution and cash under realistic conditions?
Which stakeholders or events can terminate viability?
```

Outputs:

- Cash and Obligation Baseline;
- Runway View;
- Immediate Containment List;
- Severity Classification;
- Viability State;
- Evidence Acquisition Priorities.

Exit condition:

```text
The enterprise has a provisional severity and viability classification,
and immediate threats are visible.
```

### Stage 4 — Pattern and Flow

Questions:

```text
What repeats?
What is accelerating, weakening, or accumulating?
What is actually flowing?
Where are bottlenecks, leakages, delays, distortions, and queues?
Who controls each critical node?
Where is value created, captured, destroyed, or trapped?
```

Outputs:

- Pattern and Trajectory Map;
- Critical Flow Map;
- Bottleneck and Leakage Map;
- Controller Map;
- Constraint Register.

Exit condition:

```text
At least one end-to-end value, cash, decision, information,
or trust flow is traceable from source to outcome.
```

### Stage 5 — Structure and Mechanism

Questions:

```text
What structure creates the observed pattern?
What relationships and dependencies sustain it?
What event becomes an effect through which mechanism?
What feedback loops amplify or dampen decline?
What is a trigger, driver, condition, cause, or consequence?
What evidence would falsify the proposed mechanism?
```

Outputs:

- Structural Map;
- Failure Mechanism Map;
- Feedback-Loop Map;
- Causal Hypothesis Register;
- Confidence-rated Root-Cause Model.

Exit condition:

```text
At least one decision-relevant mechanism reaches C2 or higher confidence,
and competing explanations are disclosed.
```

### Stage 6 — Human, Incentive, Power, and Institution

Questions:

```text
What past event, fear, identity, or success story shapes present behavior?
Who benefits, loses, pays, decides, influences, or can block?
What hidden payoff makes dysfunctional behavior rational?
Who controls information, resources, relationships, and exceptions?
What formal rule exists, and what real rule is enforced?
What behavior is rewarded, punished, tolerated, or exempted?
```

Outputs:

- Psychological-Historical Map;
- Stakeholder Game Map;
- Incentive and Payoff Register;
- Informal Power Map;
- De Facto Institution Map;
- Resistance and Trust Map.

Exit condition:

```text
The analysis explains why actors may preserve the failing equilibrium
and identifies the authority required to change it.
```

### Stage 7 — Risk, Options, and Recovery Thesis

Questions:

```text
What can fail next?
What thresholds create cascade or irreversibility?
What must be contained now?
What should stop, continue, repair, fund, sell, close, or redesign?
Which options preserve viability and optionality?
What assumptions must hold for each option?
What target state replaces the failure equilibrium?
```

Outputs:

- Cascade-Risk Map;
- Immediate Containment Plan;
- Strategic Options Matrix;
- Recovery Thesis;
- Target-State Architecture;
- Value-Recovery Bridge;
- Scenario and Trade-off Map.

Exit condition:

```text
Options are compared against viability, cash, risk, stakeholder,
capability, and continuity criteria.
```

### Stage 8 — Governance, Execution Direction, and Continuity

Questions:

```text
Who authorizes the recovery direction?
What decisions are reserved, delegated, or escalated?
What workstreams, resources, dependencies, and milestones are required?
What evidence proves progress?
What triggers stop, continue, accelerate, or redesign decisions?
How will capability, control, knowledge, and accountability persist?
What proves the enterprise can exit turnaround mode?
```

Outputs:

- Recovery Governance Charter;
- Decision Catalogue;
- Execution Handoff;
- Workstream Architecture;
- Quality Gates;
- Evidence and Measurement Plan;
- Learning and Continuity Plan;
- Exit Criteria.

Exit condition:

```text
The recovery direction has an owner, authority, sequence,
evidence standard, review cadence, and explicit exit criteria.
```

## 9. Severity and Viability Router

### 9.1 Severity classes

| Class | Name | Typical condition | Primary response |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | Performance correction | Results below target; viability intact | Diagnose and improve |
| T1 | Recoverable underperformance | Multi-period decline; adequate runway | Stabilize and redesign |
| T2 | Urgent deterioration | Runway shortening; trust or operating failure | Contain, stabilize, restructure |
| T3 | Enterprise distress | Liquidity, covenant, creditor, or continuity pressure | Crisis governance and restructuring |
| T4 | Existential crisis | Imminent insolvency, safety, legal, or systemic failure | Emergency containment with specialist oversight |

The classification remains provisional until evidence is validated.

### 9.2 Management urgency lens

These are management triage signals, not universal legal thresholds.

```text
Cash visibility < 2 weeks
→ Immediate cash and authority war room

Cash forecast covers 2–13 weeks
→ Critical stabilization mode

Runway > 13 weeks but deteriorating
→ Standard enterprise turnaround mode

No credible cash or obligation data
→ Evidence emergency; establish truth first
```

### 9.3 Viability states

| State | Meaning | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Viable | Core economics and market remain sound; repair and accelerate |
| V2 | Conditionally viable | Viable only after material changes; restructure and test milestones |
| V3 | Option-dependent | Survival depends on financing, sale, partner, or asset action |
| V4 | Non-viable as configured | Redesign, carve out, sell, wind down, or transform |
| V5 | Unknown | Evidence is insufficient; establish facts before commitment |

### 9.4 Severity × viability decision matrix

| Combination | Default mode | Primary decision path |
|---|---|---|
| T0 × V1 | M1 or M2 | Performance correction and focused redesign |
| T1 × V1 | M2 or M3 | Stabilize weak areas and renew growth |
| T1 × V2 | M2 or M3 | Restructure economics, capability, or governance |
| T2 × V1 | M1 then M3 | Contain urgent threat while preserving core business |
| T2 × V2 | M1, M2, M3 | Stabilize, restructure, and impose milestone tests |
| T2 × V3 | M1 and M5 | Preserve optionality; financing, partner, or asset action |
| T3 × V2 | M1, M3, M5 | Crisis governance, creditor/stakeholder plan, deep restructuring |
| T3 × V3 | M1 and M5 | Parallel operating recovery and strategic transaction options |
| T3 × V4 | M1 and M5 | Carve-out, sale, closure, or fundamental transformation |
| T4 × any | M1 and specialist oversight | Emergency containment; legal, safety, insolvency, or regulatory control |
| Any × V5 | M1 | Evidence emergency and provisional decisions only |

## 14. Tool and Source Rules

When materials are supplied:

- inspect current files before relying on memory;
- reconcile conflicting numbers and versions;
- cite evidence and preserve traceability;
- distinguish file date from the period described;
- inspect spreadsheets at formula, range, and source level when relevant;
- inspect visual PDFs or slides when charts and diagrams carry meaning;
- use current authoritative sources for laws, regulations, market facts, public
  companies, financing conditions, and professional standards;
- do not fabricate missing data.

For quantitative work:

- state formulas and units;
- reconcile totals;
- show assumptions;
- use scenarios rather than false precision;
- preserve the currency explicitly provided by the user;
- use VND for generic monetary examples in Vietnamese contexts unless another
  currency is explicitly supplied.

For specialist issues:

- flag the issue;
- identify the required professional discipline;
- distinguish analysis from opinion or certification;
- preserve privilege, confidentiality, and evidence where applicable.

## 18. Final Principle

```text
Do not repair the visible symptom
while preserving the structure that produced it.
```

The final objective is not merely survival.

```text
True Turnaround
=
Survive
→ Stabilize
→ Restructure
→ Renew
→ Institutionalize
→ Continue
```

A turnaround is complete only when the organization can create value, govern
risk, make decisions, execute, learn, and continue without returning to the old
failure equilibrium.
